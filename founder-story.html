Founder story · Built from Nigeria · Designed for the world

At 19, he started building Xiluqt.

A young Nigerian founder is attempting something unusually ambitious: an AI infrastructure layer that helps companies understand what is moving, what may go wrong, and what should happen next.

“The future will not only be about software that answers questions. It will be about systems that understand what is moving, what may go wrong, and what should happen next.”
The beginning

Starting before everything is perfect.

Drexs is 19 and building Xiluqt from Nigeria. The starting point is not a giant campus, a huge engineering organisation or a proprietary global data network. It is a working prototype and a thesis: real-world intelligence should become infrastructure — something companies can integrate into operations rather than another isolated dashboard.

The public prototype is deliberately transparent about where it stands. Its compact local risk model is trained on synthetic development scenarios. Its API is labelled BETA PROTOTYPE. Production deployment would require authorised customer data, licensed external feeds, secure backend infrastructure, evaluation against real outcomes and ongoing model calibration.

01 · WORLD GRAPH

Connect the moving world.

Shipments, orders, assets, facilities, lanes, suppliers and risk signals can be represented as one operational graph — a foundation for machine reasoning about movement.

02 · MOVEMENT INTELLIGENCE

Predict before the disruption.

The prototype estimates on-time probability, delay risk, disruption risk and confidence from structured signals, with explainability around the strongest stressor.

03 · DIGITAL TWIN

Ask “what if?” before acting.

Counterfactual scenarios explore alternate routes, inventory pressure, disruption conditions and potential business impact before a decision is executed.

04 · CONTROL TOWER

Turn intelligence into action.

A control-tower concept prioritises exceptions, recommends next steps and keeps human approval in the loop where intervention matters.

The ambition

A World Brain for movement.

Xiluqt is being developed as a broader AI infrastructure company, with product concepts spanning movement intelligence, digital twins, control-tower operations, document intelligence, freight and inventory intelligence, trust controls, an AI analyst and an API-first integration layer.

The long-term idea is simple to say and difficult to build: make the real world more legible to software. If a shipment is likely to be delayed, an inventory position is becoming dangerous, a facility is under pressure or a route is degrading, the system should help identify the signal early — then connect that intelligence to a decision.

The roadmap

Prototype → real system.

Prove. Meet customers and validate real workflows.

Fund. Secure capital for production infrastructure, data and people.

Learn. Train and calibrate against authorised real-world outcomes.

Deploy. Move the intelligence layer into secure production environments.

Why watch this?

A young founder. A very large problem.

There is no guarantee of the outcome. But the ambition is clear: build from Nigeria, design for the world, and create infrastructure that helps companies understand movement before movement becomes a problem.
